## Q1. Why and how mobile networks fail at festivals and stadiums, and what operators and venues deploy

### Takeaway
Crowds break the uplink and the signalling capacity first, not the coverage. Phones show full bars, but each cell is shared by thousands of devices, and uplink power and bandwidth run out. Operators answer with temporary cells (COWs/trucks with 20 m masts), extra spectrum, and tilt and power re-tuning. In stadiums, venues add DAS and very dense Wi-Fi 6E/7. Even so, traffic grows 20-25 % a year, and nobody guarantees service in the densest spots. An app that needs a round-trip to the cloud every few seconds will see stalls at peak moments.

### Cited Findings
**Mechanism and measurements**
- An April 2026 stadium study with commercial phones and professional drive-test tools found "a severe disparity between uplink and downlink". Networks fell back on low-band FDD for uplink. On high-band TDD, uplink transmit power sat near the 3GPP maximum while uplink quality stayed badly degraded. The authors call modern stadiums a major example of the "uplink crunch" **[academic]** — [arXiv 2604.04371, "Comprehensive Analysis of Cellular Uplink Performance in a Dense Stadium Deployment"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04371) (excerpt only, not fetched)

### Inferences
- The binding constraint for a location app is **uplink and latency at peak moments** (headliner, podium, end-of-race exodus), not downlink. A design that sends small, infrequent, compressible position updates (tens of bytes, adaptive rate) and tolerates multi-second to multi-minute stalls is far more robust than one that streams.
- Operator capacity in France and Monaco is reinforced per event, but it is opaque and not contractually available to a third-party app. ONEERA cannot count on it and cannot buy it.
- In Monaco the urban GP circuit shares the permanent dense Monaco Telecom network, plus French operators' roaming spillover from Beausoleil and Cap-d'Ail. The Principality's spectrum is tightly managed by the DPRN, so any private radio would need DPRN coordination.

### Gaps
- No published Monaco Telecom traffic or capacity figures for GP 2025 or 2026 were found, and no Orange, SFR or Bouygues traffic figures for 2025-2026 French festivals.
- No independent festival-specific (as opposed to stadium) uplink measurement study was found. Typical COW capacity (users per sector) was not found in a citable source.
- Tomorrowland, Glastonbury and Coachella guest Wi-Fi designs (AP counts, standards) were not found.

## Q2. What on-site infrastructure keeps an app working when the public internet or 4G is down, at what cost, and who does it?

### Takeaway
The proven pattern is **a venue-owned Wi-Fi network (or a private LTE/5G network for staff) with its own backhaul**: Starlink plus multi-carrier cellular bonding at greenfield sites, fibre at stadiums. A **local edge server** on that LAN can serve the app when backhaul fails. Wi-Fi rental starts at around $1k per day for small coverage, while stadium-grade high-density Wi-Fi is a multi-million capex that only permanent venues can justify. Private 5G for events exists (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile in the US; Firecell in France; Ofcom event licences in the UK), but it is used for production, payments and staff, not for consumer phones. Phone-to-phone BLE mesh is the only option that needs no infrastructure, and it is the fallback when everything else fails.

### Inferences
- **Best cost and resilience ratio for ONEERA, in tiers:**
  1. **Always on: an app that degrades gracefully.** Offline map tiles and venue map bundled in the app, last-known friend positions cached with timestamps, store-and-forward queue, and BLE proximity and mesh between friends as the zero-infrastructure fallback.
  2. **Per event, cheap: one or more edge boxes.** A mini-PC or ruggedised server running the same location/relay API, placed on the organiser's existing production or cashless LAN. Backhaul is Starlink plus 2-3 bonded SIMs on different operators. The app tries the cloud first, then a local hostname. Note that iOS restricts mDNS/local network access (a permission prompt), and captive portals break background traffic, so the realistic approach is a known private IP or local DNS on the event SSID plus an app-level "local mode". Sync to the cloud with CRDT or append-only logs once backhaul is back. Hardware plus Starlink plus SIMs is plausibly in the low thousands of euros per event (inference from the price points above, not a sourced quote).
  3. **Venue-grade: guest Wi-Fi 6E/7.** Only realistic where the venue already has it (stadiums, arenas) or where a festival builds it for cashless or production. ONEERA should **piggyback on existing organiser networks** (cashless, production, ticket scanning) rather than build its own.
  4. **Private 5G:** not useful for attendee phones (no consumer SIMs on private networks, and France's 3.8-4.2 GHz and 2.6 GHz TDD regimes are aimed at professional use). Relevant only for staff and sensors.
- Who already does "works when 4G is down": cashless and access-control vendors (Weezevent, Intellitix) run offline-tolerant terminals on private event networks; Burning Man tools use offline maps plus mesh. No consumer friend finder with an on-site edge server was found, so this is a gap and a differentiator.

### Gaps
- No published per-event cost for a festival guest Wi-Fi build in Europe, a Starlink business plan with event rental, or private 5G event pricing from Nokia or Ericsson.
- No case study found of a consumer app falling back to an on-site edge server via local DNS; the architecture above is inference.
- It is unclear whether ARCEP has a dedicated "événementiel" authorisation regime beyond case-by-case trials, and what replaced the 3.8-4.0 GHz window after Dec 2025 beyond the 3.8-4.2 GHz rules. Monaco's equivalent (DPRN) private-network rules were not found.

## Q3. Case studies and lessons from existing event apps (friend finding, indoor positioning, crowd analytics)

### Takeaway
Festival friend finders have existed since at least 2015 (Coachella), and Glastonbury's Vodafone app offered live location sharing in 2025. They all rely on mutual opt-in between friends and on GPS plus cellular, so they degrade exactly when crowds peak. Beacon programmes (Coachella 2014, Bonnaroo 2015) show that opt-in can be high when asked during onboarding, but engagement is low. Indoor positioning vendors quote 1-5 m accuracy with no independent stadium figures. The official F1 and ACM apps offer maps and wayfinding, not friend location.

### Inferences
- **Lessons for ONEERA:**
  - Ask for location and Bluetooth **during onboarding, tied to a clear user benefit** ("find your friends"). Vendor data suggests 60-80 % opt-in, but expect lower in 2026 because of iOS and Android permission granularity (precise vs approximate, "while using", and Android 12+ "nearby devices").
  - Friend finding must be **mutual and time-boxed** (Coachella model) to avoid creepiness.
  - Do not rely on beacon push marketing; engagement was about 20 % at Bonnaroo.
  - Accuracy claims of 1-3 m are vendor-stated in quiet buildings. In dense crowds, bodies attenuate BLE and Wi-Fi, so plan for 3-10 m and validate on site.
  - In Monaco the circuit is an outdoor urban canyon (harbour, buildings, tunnel), so GNSS multipath is the main issue. ACM and F1 already cover maps and wayfinding, which leaves **friend location** as the uncovered niche.
- Battery: festival-goers prize their phones and charging is scarce, so the app must budget for multi-day use (adaptive GPS duty cycling, BLE advertising at low duty cycle).

## Q4. Regulatory context (France/EU/Monaco) for location tracking and temporary private radio networks

### Takeaway
In France, consent-based app location sharing falls under GDPR and ePrivacy, with CNIL's 2026 location-data recommendation as the main guidance. Passive Wi-Fi or Bluetooth crowd counting is governed by CNIL's "mesure d'audience et de fréquentation" doctrine (notice at entrances, opt-out, anonymisation). In Monaco, the relevant law is now **Loi n° 1.565 of 3 Dec 2024**, which replaced the old Loi n° 1.165 and created the APDP. Private radio needs ARCEP (France) or DPRN (Monaco) authorisation.

### Cited Findings
- CNIL published a final **recommendation on the use of location data** (PDF dated June 2026), after a March 2025 draft for consultation. The excerpts seen were only the table of contents **[regulator]** — [CNIL recommendation 2026](https://cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-06/recommandation_utilisation_de_donnees_de_localisation.pdf); [Draft March 2025](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/cnil/files/2025-03/projet_de_recommandation_relative_aux_traitements_de_donnees_de_localisation.pdf)
- CNIL guidance on geolocation and mobile apps: location data is personal data when it can identify someone directly or indirectly. A location history alone can single out a person (for example by revealing their home) **[regulator]** — [CNIL: géolocalisation et applications mobiles](https://cnil.fr/fr/geolocalisation-applications-mobiles-quelles-regles); [CNIL: protéger vos données](https://cnil.fr/fr/geolocalisation-applications-mobiles-proteger-vos-donnees)
- CNIL on audience and footfall measurement in public places: notice at entry and exit points and on Wi-Fi APs; an explicit opt-out network name (e.g. "wifi_tracking_optout"); controllers must not tell people to switch off Wi-Fi to avoid tracking **[regulator, via law-firm summary]** — [CNIL page](https://cnil.fr/fr/dispositifs-de-mesure-daudience-et-de-frequentation-dans-des-espaces-accessibles-au-public-la-cnil); [Hunton summary](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/cnil-details-rules-audience-traffic-measuring-publicly-accessible-areas)
- CNIL 2026 recommendation on tracking pixels (relevant to analytics SDKs in the app) — [CNIL PDF April 2026](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-04/recommandation-pixels_de_suivi.pdf)
- Monaco: **Loi n° 1.565 of 3 Dec 2024** is GDPR-inspired. It applies extraterritorially to controllers outside Monaco that process data of people on Monaco territory, which covers a foreign app used at the GP. It creates the **APDP** (replacing the CCIN), requires 72 h breach notification and a DPO in some cases. Fines are up to €10 M per CMS; Clym says €10 M or 4 % of turnover (conflict, unverified against the official text). Implementing **Ordonnance Souveraine n° 11.327 of 10 July 2025** — [CMS Monaco (FR)](https://cms.law/fr/mco/publication/nouvelle-loi-monegasque-sur-la-protection-des-donnees-personnelles); [CMS (EN)](https://cms.law/en/mco/publication/the-new-monegasque-data-protection-law); [KPMG Monaco 2025 No4](https://kpmg.com/mc/fr/home/newsletters/newsletters-2025-no4/kpmg-monaco_newsletter-2025-no4_loi_1565_securite_donnees_notification_violations.html); [KPMG Apr 2026](https://kpmg.com/mc/fr/kpmg-monaco-news/2026/04/loi-n-1-565-protection-donnees-Monaco.html); [Clym](https://www.clym.io/regulations/law-on-the-protection-of-personal-information-monaco); [CMS expert guide](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-data-protection-and-cyber-security-laws/monaco)
- Radio: in France, ARCEP 2.6 GHz TDD and 3.8-4.2 GHz regimes (see Q2). In Monaco, DPRN plus ANFR coordination of about 750 frequencies for the GP (see Q1).

### Inferences
- The brief mentions "Loi n° 1.165 amended 2023/2024". That law has been **superseded** by Loi n° 1.565 (Dec 2024), so ONEERA's legal docs should cite 1.565, the APDP and OS 11.327.
- Friend-to-friend sharing with explicit consent is the low-risk model. Organiser-facing crowd heat-maps derived from app users need aggregation and anonymisation thresholds plus a separate consent purpose. Any passive Wi-Fi or BLE sniffing of non-users falls under the stricter audience-measurement doctrine.
- An on-site edge server holding location data makes the organiser and/or ONEERA controllers or processors on-site. It needs retention limits (purge after the event) and security, since the 72 h breach rule applies in both France and Monaco.

### Gaps
- The full content of the CNIL 2026 location recommendation (retention durations, precision guidance) was not read.
- No CNIL or APDP decision specific to festivals or sports events was found.
- Monaco's licensing procedure for temporary private LTE/5G (DPRN) was not found.
