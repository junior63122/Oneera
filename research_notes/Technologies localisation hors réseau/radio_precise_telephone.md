# High-precision radio indoor positioning on consumer smartphones (status late 2026)

Scope: UWB (Nearby Interaction / Android Ranging / FiRa DL-TDoA), Bluetooth 6.x Channel Sounding, Bluetooth 5.1 AoA/AoD, Wi-Fi RTT (802.11mc/az), BLE RSSI + PDR baseline. Context: event app (festivals, clubs, stadiums, Monaco GP), thousands of users in dense crowds, indoor + outdoor, users' own phones.

Research caveat: several primary pages could not be fetched from this session (network allowlist blocked firaconsortium.org, community.cisco.com, arxiv.org, estimote.com, themobileknowledge.com). Facts from those domains come only from search-result snippets and are flagged "(snippet)". Apple WWDC26 session and Android Ranging docs were fetched in full.

## 1. Which technologies can a mass-market festival audience use on their own phones in 2026, without special hardware?

### Takeaway
The only technology that works on essentially 100% of iPhones and Android phones today is BLE RSSI beacons (+ PDR/sensor fusion), at metre-to-several-metre accuracy that degrades in crowds. The big 2026 change is UWB Downlink-TDoA ("untracked navigation", phone only listens to fixed anchors, so it scales to unlimited users): it became a public API on Android 17 (Ranging module, `DEVICE_ROLE_DT_TAG`, background allowed with background-location permission) and, per vendor/news sources, in Apple Nearby Interaction on iOS 27 — but it only covers the UWB-equipped share of phones (all recent iPhones, only premium Android) and real venue deployments are still pilots/vendor claims. Bluetooth Channel Sounding is in phones now (Pixel 9/10, iPhone 17 with N1, Galaxy S26+/Ultra) but is a 1-to-1 connected-ranging technique, foreground-only on iOS, and does not scale to thousands of users around fixed anchors. Wi-Fi RTT is Android-only (no iPhone API found). BLE AoD is not supported by phones.

### Cited Findings

**UWB — Android**
- Android 16 (API 36) introduced a unified Ranging module (`RangingManager`) covering UWB, Bluetooth Channel Sounding, Wi-Fi NAN RTT and Bluetooth RSSI ranging; Android 17 (API 37) adds Wi-Fi Proximity Detection and UWB Downlink-TDoA — [Android Developers: Range between devices](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/ranging)
- In a DL-TDoA session the phone takes role `DEVICE_ROLE_DT_TAG` and "receive[s] signals from multiple UWB anchors"; capability checked with `uwbCapabilities.isDlTdoaSupported`; results arrive via `onDlTdoaResults(DlTdoaMeasurement)`; config can be loaded from a FiRa config packet advertised over Wi-Fi vendor IE or BLE service data (profile "Untracked Navigation") — [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/ranging)
- DL-TDoA requires `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` (not the `RANGING` permission) and `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION` to range in background — [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/ranging)
- Background rule: "Third-party apps are only allowed to perform background ranging with ultra-wideband, and only on supported devices. Ranging in the background with other technologies is not allowed." Ranging can also be refused when the max number of concurrent sessions is reached or for battery/performance reasons — [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/ranging)
- Android Ranging module supports UWB interop with iOS (unicast DS-TWR matching Apple's Nearby Interaction Accessory Protocol) — [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/ranging)
- Samsung: UWB only on Galaxy S26+ and S26 Ultra (not base S26) per device lists — [Wikipedia: List of UWB-enabled mobile devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_UWB-enabled_mobile_devices); [SammyGuru](https://sammyguru.com/which-galaxy-phones-support-uwb/). One UI 9 (Android 17) rolling out to S26/S25 from late Sept 2026 — [9to5Google, 29 Sep 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/09/29/samsung-android-17-update-rolling-out-galaxy-s26-s25/). No source found confirming Samsung exposes `isDlTdoaSupported = true`.

**Wi-Fi RTT**
- Android RTT API since Android 9; requires AP implementing 802.11-2016 FTM (mc) or 802.11az non-trigger-based; az enabled on Android 15+ devices, fallback to mc otherwise — [Android Developers Wi-Fi RTT](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/wifi/wifi-rtt); [AOSP Wi-Fi RTT](https://source.android.com/docs/core/connect/wifi-rtt)

- iPhone: no public Wi-Fi RTT/FTM API found in any source searched (gap, see below).

**BLE AoA/AoD**
- AoD puts the antenna array on the transmitter and a single antenna on the receiver (phone), but "at this time smartphones do not support Bluetooth Direction Finding" — [Nordic Direction Finding](https://www.nordicsemi.com/Products/Low-power-short-range-wireless/Direction-finding); SIG frames phone support as future ("once smartphone vendors integrate...") — [Bluetooth SIG Direction Finding](https://bluetooth.com/learn-about-bluetooth/feature-enhancements/direction-finding)
- Quuppa claims phones can be tracked by AoA locators via "target emulation" (phone emits BLE advertising the locators can process) — [Wiliot podcast with Quuppa](https://www.wiliot.com/podcasts/high-precision-location-angle-of-arrival-quuppa-part-2) (vendor claim, unverified)

**BLE RSSI (iOS constraints)**
- iOS: 20 monitored beacon regions per app (a region can be UUID-only and cover thousands of beacons); CLMonitor (iOS 17) uses BeaconIdentityCondition, limits believed similar; region relaunch about once every 3–5 min for back-to-back events; device-wide priority group of ~20–30 regions inferred by one vendor — [Apple forums 736099](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/736099); [Estimote forum](https://forums.estimote.com/t/is-there-any-limitation-to-monitor-more-than-40-beacons-in-ios-sdk-at-a-time/769); [Flybuy](https://www.flybuy.com/?p=157); iOS 27 beta report of missed CLMonitor enter/exit events — [Apple forums 838021](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/838021)

### Inferences
- Realistic tiering for an Oneera-type audience in 2026: (a) universal baseline = BLE RSSI + PDR + GNSS outdoors on every phone; (b) premium precision layer = UWB DL-TDoA on UWB phones (all iPhone 11+ hardware has UWB, but DL-TDoA API availability/model support on iOS 27 needs Apple-doc confirmation; on Android only flagship models with Android 17 and OEM enablement). In France/Monaco iPhone share is high, so UWB DL-TDoA could cover a large minority-to-majority of attendees — share figures not researched here.
- Channel Sounding is a "find my tag / proximity to a connected peer" technology on phones (iPhone = initiator only, accessory must be AccessorySetupKit-paired, foreground only). Ranging thousands of phones against fixed CS anchors would require each phone to hold a connection to several anchors simultaneously; nothing in the docs suggests this is supported at scale. Best fit: friend-finding phone-to-phone or meet-up point ("you are 4.2 m from the bar sign") in foreground, not venue-wide positioning.
- Background: only Android UWB (incl. DL-TDoA with background location) is officially background-capable; iOS UWB background needs paired accessory or a Live Activity (iOS 18.4+); CS is foreground-only on both platforms. Background behaviour of iOS DL-TDoA is unknown.

## 2. What accuracy is actually measured (lab vs field, crowds/body blocking)?

### Takeaway
UWB and Channel Sounding deliver decimetre-class ranging in favourable conditions, but independent tests show CS degrades sharply with PCB antennas/range and both suffer from body and NLOS blocking; no independent field study of phone DL-TDoA in a dense crowd was found. Wi-Fi RTT on phones gives ~1–1.3 m in offices. BLE RSSI in real crowds is 4–11 m (95th pct) and IPIN smartphone-track winners have historically been ~1–4 m third-quartile error.

**UWB**
- DL-TDoA requires ≥4 anchors with good signal at every point of the floor map — [Cisco Spaces: Deploying APs for optimal UWB performance](https://spaces.cisco.com/beyond-connectivity-deploying-for-location-success/); same point in [Cisco community blog](https://community.cisco.com/t5/networking-blogs/precision-meets-scale-cisco-brings-uwb-to-indoor-location/bc-p/5361054) (snippet)
- Eforthink (June 2026 press release) claims "centimeter-level" DL-TDoA positioning on UWB phones, validated on iOS and Android, targeting malls, airports, museums, stadiums — no named customer — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/eforthink-launches-indoor-navigation-and-spatial-services-for-uwb-enabled-smartphones-302803804.html) (marketing claim)
- iOS 26 beta tester: iPhone 16 Pro returned distance-only, iPhone 13 distance+direction in DL-TDoA tests — [Apple forums 799436](https://www.developer.apple.com/forums/thread/799436) (snippet, anecdotal)
- Independent smartphone UWB ranging evaluation exists ("Smartphones with UWB: Evaluating the Accuracy and Reliability of UWB Ranging", arXiv 2303.11220) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.11220) — content not retrievable here.

**Bluetooth Channel Sounding**

**Wi-Fi RTT**
- Android 2D static ±1.2 m, dynamic ±1.3 m; Pixel 2 office ~1.20 m mean — [IEEE 9151400 / UCL](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/9151400/)
- WiNar (RTT+RSS fusion) <0.77 m mean on two testbeds; negative ranges near APs — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1574119221000687)

**BLE AoA (Quuppa etc.)**
- Vendor: "a few centimeters"; independent-style review: 0.2–1 m typical in moderate environments, strongly dependent on locator density and RF design — [rfp.wiki Quuppa review](https://www.rfp.wiki/vendors/quuppa)

**BLE RSSI baseline + crowds**
- Crowd presence changed RSSI by up to 8.6 dBm, coverage by up to 24 m, proximity accuracy by 18 %, distance-estimation accuracy by up to 41 % — [Sensors / sdl.edu.sa record](https://sindex.sdl.edu.sa/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Using-Bluetooth-Low-Energy-for-positioning/9931310508331) (snippet)
- Body covering a phone lowers RSSI ~5–8 dB — [PMC5421664](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5421664) (snippet)
- Field test at an indoor fair (TU Dresden): fingerprinting best when empty but ~11 m (95th pct) in crowds; weighted centroid ~5.7 m; ~1 beacon / 40 m² is the point of diminishing returns — [TU Dresden](https://fis.tu-dresden.de/portal/en/publications/trilateration-fingerprinting-and-centroid-taking-indoor-positioning-with-bluetooth-le-to-the-wild(3ec3129d-7353-49eb-b0e2-1d26884e1b54).html) (snippet)
- Body-shadowing-aware BLE: 3.7 m / 6.7 m at 90 % vs 8.7 m / 12.3 m for fingerprinting/classic — [U Calgary thesis](https://ucalgary.scholaris.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/9aa171df-28fd-4b19-92cb-9f905d44c1fb/content); IMU-aided body-shadowing compensation improved accuracy ~60 % — [MDPI Sensors 18(1):304](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/18/1/304/htm)
- IPIN competition smartphone tracks (third-quartile error): 2017 winner 3.48 m — [PMC5855053](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5855053/); 2019 on-site 3.8 m, off-site 2.3 m — [IPIN 2019 results](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/the-ipin-2019-indoor-localisation-competition-description-and-results/wrk_a1c24f205ad7a1b53ae9ebafaaee8cab); 2020 best ~1 m Q3 — [IEEE 9439493](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/9439493/); 2021–22 on-site Track 1 best 11.7 m (data quality caveat) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377767351_Offsite_evaluation_of_localization_systems_criteria_systems_and_results_from_IPIN_2021-22_competitions)

### Inferences
- In a packed festival pit, 2.4 GHz (BLE/CS) and 6–8 GHz (UWB) are both heavily absorbed by bodies; UWB TDoA with phone in pocket/held low among crowd should be expected to lose LOS to many anchors — mounting anchors high (truss, lighting towers) is the main mitigation. Expect field accuracy closer to 0.5–2 m than vendor "cm-level" until proven in a trial.
- BLE RSSI+PDR remains realistic at 3–6 m in crowds with dense beacons and body-shadowing compensation; good for zone-level (stage, bar, gate) but not for "which side of the person next to me".

### Gaps
- No independent measurement of smartphone DL-TDoA accuracy in a real crowd/event found; no 2023–2025 IPIN results retrieved; no Microsoft Indoor Localization Competition results retrieved; no controlled phone-CS body-blocking study.

## 3. Infrastructure, cost per venue, install time, battery, scalability, maturity, deployments

### Takeaway
DL-TDoA UWB needs a synchronized anchor grid (≥4 anchors in view everywhere) — component cost ~$30–60 per UWB module, but a finished anchor with enclosure/power/backhaul/install costs much more (no public per-m² prices found); Cisco now ships Wi-Fi 7 APs with UWB radios, mainly used today for AP self-location. Scalability: DL-TDoA (phones passive) and BLE beacons (phones passive) scale to unlimited users; TWR UWB, CS and Wi-Fi RTT require per-user exchanges and are airtime-limited. Outdoor fixed UWB anchors in the EU are legal since the 2024/1467 amendment under specific conditions.

- Rough sizing (inference, not sourced): if a DL-TDoA anchor covers ~20–30 m radius and ≥4 must be visible everywhere, a 10,000 m² festival pit might need several dozen anchors; at module cost ~$40–60 plus enclosure, battery/PoE, sync and labour, a finished anchor likely lands in the low hundreds of euros each — validate with Estimote/Eforthink/Qorvo quotes. Temporary installs on truss with battery power are plausible within a day if anchors self-survey (Cisco-style AP auto-locate shows the principle).
- Battery: DL-TDoA receive-only is the lightest precise mode in principle (phone never transmits UWB), but continuous UWB RX still costs power; arXiv 2402.10515 work on power-efficient DL-TDoA indicates it is a recognized issue. No measured mAh/hour figures found.
- Monaco GP: street circuit = outdoor fixed installation → falls under the 2024/1467 fixed-outdoor category (conditions apply); Monaco is not EU but follows CEPT/French spectrum practice — check with Direction des Communications Électroniques de Monaco (not researched).
- Maturity ranking for crowd-scale venue positioning on users' phones (2026): BLE RSSI (mature, universal, low accuracy) > UWB DL-TDoA (APIs just shipped on Android 17 / iOS 27, ecosystem pilots, highest potential) > Wi-Fi RTT (Android-only, niche AP support) > Channel Sounding (phones ready, but peer/foreground model unsuited) > BLE AoA (needs tag or unproven phone emulation; tracks phone from infrastructure — privacy/consent and does not give the phone its own position).
