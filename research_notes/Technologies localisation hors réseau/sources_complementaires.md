# Complementary (non-beacon) smartphone positioning sources for event venues, as of late 2026

Scope: consumer phones only. Covers GNSS (dual-frequency, raw measurements, Galileo HAS, PPP), 5G/LTE network positioning, visual positioning (VPS), magnetic fingerprinting, PDR / learned inertial odometry, barometer floor detection, and iOS/Android background limits. Research date 2026-11-26. Vendor claims are marked "(vendor claim)". Sources dated before 2025 are marked "(older)".

## Which complementary sources materially help in a dense outdoor festival or on a street circuit like Monaco, and which are hype?

### Takeaway
Only two sources reliably help on every phone: GNSS (dual-frequency where available, plus Google's 3D-mapping-aided correction on Android) and inertial PDR/sensor fusion. Expect roughly 5–10 m in urban canyons; this is well short of a "which friend is next to me" resolution. Galileo HAS/PPP reaches sub-metre to 1 m only in open sky, only after about 2 minutes of convergence, and only on Android through custom raw-measurement processing. Under grandstands and in Monaco-type canyons it is not a reliable gain. VPS (ARCore Geospatial, Niantic VPS 2.0, ARKit geo-anchors) is accurate when it can localize. It is impractical for continuous friend tracking: it needs the camera raised, the internet, and an unoccluded view, which a crowd blocks. Treat it as an occasional "recalibrate me" tool, not a tracker. 5G network positioning (PRS, Rel-16/17/18) is not usable by third-party apps in 2026 and depends on the very network that is saturated, so it is hype for this use case. Magnetic fingerprinting is only plausible indoors (clubs, stadium concourses) and is fragile around temporary metal structures.

### Cited Findings

**GNSS: dual-frequency and urban canyons**
- Hong Kong urban canyons (older, 2021): adding smartphone L5 pseudoranges to 3D-mapping-aided GNSS gave about 10 m average accuracy, better than L1-only. The authors credit L5's shorter chip length with reducing multipath. — [Ng et al., NAVI/ION](https://navi.ion.org/content/68/4/727); [PolyU full paper](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/aae/ipn-lab/us/publications/Fullpaper/Urban%20Positioning_3D%20Mapping%20Aided%20GNSS%20using%20Dual-Frequency%20Pseudorange%20Measurements%20from%20Smartphones.pdf)
- A PolyU summary says the same 3D-mapping-aided dual-frequency approach can reliably reach under 5 m in Hong Kong canyons. This is a research result, not a product figure. — [PolyU summary](https://polyu.edu.hk/pair/publications/issue-3/kt3---urban-positioning)
- Xiaomi Mi 8, the first dual-frequency phone (older, 2019), in open sky: about 4.57 m horizontal DRMS on E5 against about 5.36 m on E1/L1. Open-sky, not urban. — [Robustelli et al. 2019](https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/e383531e-ab38-15e8-e053-a505fe0a3de9/Robustelli_Assessment-dual-frequency_2019.pdf)
- A 2026 arXiv study of smartphone GNSS in urban, suburban, indoor and driving conditions found that the operating environment, more than the device hardware, is the main driver of error. Its accuracy figures are the phones' self-reported estimates, not errors against a surveyed reference. — [arXiv 2603.26706](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26706v1)
- Google's 3D-mapping-aided GNSS (older, 2020) ships in Google Play services and feeds the Fused Location Provider using 3D building tiles for more than 3,850 cities. Version 1 cut wrong-side-of-street errors by about 50% on Android 8+ phones; version 2 (Pixel 5/4a 5G) cut them by about 75%. It was pedestrian-only at launch. I found no newer public figures. — [Android Developers Blog, Dec 2020](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2020/12/improving-urban-gps-accuracy-for-your.html?hl=en)
- iPhone: Apple's iPhone 17 spec page lists "precision dual-frequency GPS" (GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, QZSS, BeiDou, NavIC). GSMArena gives L1+L5 for the iPhone 17 (A3258). — [Apple iPhone 17 specs](https://www.apple.com/sa/iphone-17/specs/); [GSMArena](https://m.gsmarena.com/_iphone_17-14050.php)
- Galileo is native in smartphones: EUSPA reports 4.5 billion Galileo-enabled smartphones in use in 2024. — [EUSPA, GNSS and your phone](https://www.euspa.europa.eu/gnss-and-your-phone)

**GNSS: Galileo HAS and PPP on phones**
- Raw GNSS measurements have been available on Android since 7.0 (2016). Google Pixel 7 Pro + Galileo HAS + the raPPPid software (TU Wien) reached decimetre-level accuracy "under good conditions"; the poster shows 2D error under 1 m and 3D around 1 m. — [EGU24 abstract](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU24/EGU24-9332.html); [TU Wien poster PDF](https://repositum.tuwien.at/bitstream/20.500.12708/206047/1/Wareyka-Glaner-2024-Smartphone%20PPP%20with%20the%20Galileo%20High%20Accuracy%20Service-vor.pdf)
- "Breaking the one-meter level" (older, 2023): Samsung Galaxy S23+ about 1 m horizontal and Pixel 7 a few decimetres with HAS corrections, after about 2 minutes of convergence in quasi-real-time. — [MDPI Eng. Proc. 54(1):16](https://mdpi.com/2673-4591/54/1/16)
- Google's GPS.gov advisory talk (van Diggelen, Dec 2023, older): Galileo HAS and GDGPS gave similar accuracy across 25 drive traces with 7 phone models, measured against a carrier-phase reference. — [GPS.gov presentation](https://www.GPS.gov/governance/advisory/meetings/2023-12/van-diggelen.pdf)
- A York University dissertation found smartphone navigation with HAS corrections highly comparable to PPP using CNES products in vehicle tests. — [YorkSpace](https://yorkspace.library.yorku.ca/bitstreams/0f05a19e-fb38-4732-b7fc-6d05e0970d81/download)
- I found no source showing iOS or Android exposing HAS natively in the platform location API. The published results all use raw measurements plus third-party PPP software. — searched [EUSPA FAQ](https://www.euspa.europa.eu/eu-space-programme/galileo/faqs/can-i-use-galileo-my-mobile-phone) and [GSC](https://www.gsc-europa.eu/node/7577)

**GNSS: crowds**
- Human bodies and bags attenuate GNSS signals, as noted in crowdsourced-GNSS literature, but I found no dB figure at L1. — [arXiv 2106.00107](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00107)
- Proxies at other frequencies (not GNSS): a body covering a phone lowers 2.4 GHz RSS by about 5–8 dB, with more people causing more attenuation ([PMC5421664](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5421664/)). A person blocking a 26 GHz line-of-sight link costs a median 5.9 dB ([arXiv 2404.05477](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.05477)).

**5G/LTE network positioning**
- Rel-18 (frozen mid-2024) adds RedCap positioning, PRS bandwidth aggregation, UE-based carrier-phase positioning and sidelink positioning (SL-PRS, SLPP). — [Ericsson blog, Nov 2024](https://www.ericsson.com/en/blog/2024/11/5g-advanced-positioning-in-3gpp-release-18); [arXiv 2401.17594](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17594v1)
- These are network/LMF-centric procedures over LPP. I found no evidence of an Android or iOS app-level API exposing PRS measurements, and no European operator announcing commercial PRS positioning for consumer apps. — [3GPP NR positioning overview](https://3gpp.org/technologies/nr-positioning-2)

**Visual positioning (VPS)**
- ARCore Geospatial blends VPS and GPS, and VPS is the main source of precision in dense urban areas. Google's iOS docs give typical VPS accuracy of about 5 m position and 5° rotation. The Jetpack XR page claims sub-metre / sub-degree when VPS localizes (vendor claim, XR devices). Requirements: internet (it calls the ARCore API service), fine location, a qualifying magnetometer (not every ARCore device qualifies), and a Google Cloud project. Coverage relies on Street View imagery. — [ARCore Geospatial enable/quickstart](https://developers.google.com/ar/develop/java/geospatial/enable); [VPS availability](https://developers.google.com/ar/develop/java/geospatial/check-vps-availability); [Jetpack XR Geospatial](https://developer.android.com/develop/xr/jetpack-xr-sdk/arcore/geospatial)
- Niantic Spatial (spun off from Niantic, which sold its games to Scopely in 2025) launched VPS 2.0 and Scaniverse on 7 April 2026. It claims "near-centimetre" 6DoF in Scaniverse-mapped places and a global-scale coarser VPS elsewhere (vendor claim; no independent verification found). NSDK 4.0 covers Unity, Swift, Android and ROS 2. The company is pivoting toward robotics and defence (US Army DevX "awardable", June 2026) and a Singapore national VPS MoU (September 2026). — [Niantic Spatial VPS 2.0 blog](https://nianticspatial.com/blog/vps2); [GeekWire 2026](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/from-pokemon-go-to-physical-ai-niantic-spatial-unveils-its-global-3d-mapping-platform/); [Robotics247](https://www.robotics247.com/article/niantic-spatial-launches-scaniverse-3d-space-capture-vps-2.0-virtual-positioning-platforms/technologies); [Army DevX](https://nianticspatial.com/blog/army-devx-awardable); [MoguraVR, Singapore](https://www.moguravr.com/niantic-spatial-singapore-vps/)
- Niantic Spatial's own blog promotes VPS for GPS-denied environments (vendor framing). — [Niantic Spatial blog](https://nianticspatial.com/blog/gps-denied-environments)
- Apple ARKit ARGeoTrackingConfiguration (location anchors): only in Apple-mapped cities (checked at runtime with `checkAvailability`), outdoors only, A12+ chip, and it downloads localization data, so it needs the network. The fetch returned only the page title, so these points come from general knowledge and need verification. — [Apple ARGeoTrackingConfiguration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/argeotrackingconfiguration)

**Magnetic fingerprinting**
- IndoorAtlas claims 0.1–2 m in modern buildings with the phone magnetometer only (vendor claim). A third-party vendor comparison lists 1–3 m and warns that magnetic signatures drift over time and depend on map quality; that blog is internally inconsistent in places. — [CrowdConnected 2026 comparison](https://www.crowdconnected.com/blog/best-indoor-positioning-systems/); [Wikipedia, Magnetic positioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_positioning)
- Research on induced-field methods reports under 20 cm in 2D, but these are a different technique from ambient-field fingerprinting. — [arXiv 2602.00817](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00817v1)

**PDR, learned inertial odometry, barometer**
- RoNIN (older) built a benchmark of more than 40 hours from 100 subjects with phone IMUs. TLIO reduced yaw and position drift by 27% and 33% compared with the best RoNIN baseline (relative figures). — [RoNIN arXiv 1905.12853](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.12853); [TLIO arXiv 2007.01867](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.01867)
- 2026 comparisons put TLIO at about 2.5 m ATE (TLIO dataset) and 4.1 m (Nymeria), on head-mounted devices. A 2026 differentiable-filtering paper shows RoNIN and TLIO still suffer significant translational drift on smartphone held-out sequences. — [HeadIO arXiv 2611.14895](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14895); [arXiv 2611.13517](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13517); [GNIO arXiv 2603.15281](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15281)
- Barometer floor detection: floor heights can fall within a phone barometer's error, and weather drift dominates. Approaches use reference barometers or entrance-relative pressure; one study reached 91% within ±1 floor, and no method was found to approach 100%. — [Microsoft Research HotMobile'14](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/hotmobile14b.pdf); [Sensors 23(1):80](https://mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/1/80); [PMC6720727](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6720727/); [arXiv 1710.11122](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.11122v1)

**Background execution (continuous friend tracking)**
- Android: a foreground service using location must declare type `location` and the FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION permission. Since Android 14, a while-in-use location FGS cannot be started from the background unless the app holds ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION or another exemption applies. Android 15 FGS timeouts apply only to dataSync and mediaProcessing, not location. — [Android FGS changes](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/changes); [FGS types required (Android 14)](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/fgs-types-required); [FGS timeout](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fg-service-timeout)
- iOS: `CLLocationUpdate.liveUpdates()` plus `CLBackgroundActivitySession` (iOS 17+) keep background updates alive, with automatic pause when the user is stationary. A session can only be started in the foreground. Developers report a Live Activity does not substitute for the session. Developer reports also describe deliveries stopping after 2–5 minutes on iOS 27 in some configurations (anecdotal). — [WWDC23 session 10180](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10180); [Apple forum 788647](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/788647); [Apple forum 844579](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/844579); [Apple forum 767460](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/767460)

### Inferences
- **Monaco street circuit:** narrow streets, 5–10-storey buildings, grandstands and tunnel sections are a classic urban canyon. Expect single-phone GNSS in the 5–15 m class with frequent multipath jumps, and dropouts under grandstand roofs and in the tunnel. L5 and Google's 3D-mapping-aided GNSS help on the street-side choice. Galileo HAS/PPP gains largely vanish there, because PPP convergence needs continuous carrier-phase lock, which canyons and bodies break. This inference rests on the canyon and convergence findings above, not on a Monaco-specific test.
- **Open-air festival:** open sky makes GNSS decent (about 3–5 m), but a dense crowd and a phone in a pocket add attenuation (likely several dB, by analogy with the 2.4 GHz figures) and degrade it. PDR helps smooth and bridge gaps. VPS adds little in a field with few distinctive, stable structures and many moving people.
- **Indoor clubs and stadium concourses:** GNSS is unusable. Magnetic fingerprinting plus PDR is the only beacon-free option, and it needs a site survey each time. Stages, trusses and LED walls are temporary and likely invalidate fingerprints near them.
- **Hype for this use case:** 5G PRS (no app access, and it depends on the saturated network), VPS as a continuous tracker (camera must be up and online, crowd occlusion), and centimetre-level "HAS/RTK on phones" (lab and open-sky results).
- **Material help:** dual-frequency GNSS (now on the iPhone 17 base model and most mid/high Android phones), the platform fused location, PDR/IMU fusion for smoothing and short gaps, the barometer for level changes (relative, between the entrance and the current position), and an optional, opportunistic VPS "snap" at a few landmark spots.
- For friend tracking, background limits matter more than the positioning technique. On iOS the session must be started while the app is in the foreground at the event. On Android, use a `location`-type foreground service with a persistent notification. Either way, plan on 1–10 s update cadence, not sub-second.

## What works with zero internet connectivity on the phone?

### Takeaway
GNSS works fully offline once the receiver has an almanac and ephemeris. A cold start without A-GNSS takes tens of seconds; predicted-orbit assistance (PSDS/XTRA) downloaded beforehand keeps first fixes fast for several days. PDR/IMU, the barometer, and magnetic fingerprinting with a pre-downloaded map also work offline. Cloud VPS (ARCore Geospatial, ARKit geo-anchors, Niantic VPS) and Galileo HAS via internet do not work offline, and neither does network/5G positioning. HAS is also broadcast on the Galileo E6 signal, but phones do not receive E6, so phone HAS needs internet-delivered corrections.

### Cited Findings
- PSDS (Predicted Satellite Data Service, formerly Qualcomm XTRA) provides long-term orbit predictions, usually weekly, that allow fast TTFF for several days without an internet connection. Injection is supported on Android 10+. Some devices do not support PSDS, and periodic background download is off by default in AOSP and depends on the vendor build. — [Replicant GNSS research](https://redmine.replicant.us/projects/replicant/wiki/GNSSResearch); [/e/OS AOSP commit](https://gitlab.e.foundation/e/os/android_frameworks_base/-/commit/b2fca14b6fe91696cf931142baf945ab65a66149); [GSC GPSTest guide](https://www.gsc-europa.eu/sites/default/files/sites/all/files/understanding_gnss_performance_on_android_using_the_gps_testc_app.pdf)
- Stored predicted navigation models shorten TTFF without a data connection at positioning time (HERE's general description). — [HERE predicted GNSS data](https://docs.here.com/positioning/docs/here-gnss-data-api-v2-concepts-predicted-gnss-data)
- ARCore Geospatial requires network access to the ARCore API service, and its accuracy depends on internet connectivity. — [ARCore Geospatial enable](https://developers.google.com/ar/develop/java/geospatial/enable)
- Smartphone HAS research uses corrections streamed over the internet with raw measurements; phones track E1/E5a, not E6. — [EGU24](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU24/EGU24-9332.html); [MDPI 2023](https://mdpi.com/2673-4591/54/1/16)
- Magnetic fingerprinting uses only the phone magnetometer at runtime (vendor claim), so it can run offline if the venue map is cached; whether the SDK allows this is not confirmed. — [CrowdConnected](https://www.crowdconnected.com/blog/best-indoor-positioning-systems/)

### Inferences
- **Offline-capable stack:** GNSS (with A-GNSS/PSDS assistance pre-fetched when the user opens the app before the event), PDR/IMU fusion on the device, the barometer, the venue map bundled in the app, plus any beacon or peer layers covered by other researchers.
- **On iOS,** Core Location's GNSS works offline. Wi-Fi and cell-based location components may degrade without a database fetch, but GNSS does not need one.
- **Friends' positions** need a transport, which is out of scope here (BLE mesh, Wi-Fi Aware, LoRa and so on). Positioning itself can be offline; sharing it cannot be done over the saturated cellular data network.
- **Battery:** no measured figures found. My inference is that continuous GNSS at 1 Hz with fused location costs a meaningful amount of battery over a multi-hour event, while camera-on VPS is the costliest and is unsuitable for continuous use.

### Gaps
- No measured offline cold or warm TTFF figures for 2025–2026 phones were found.
- I did not verify whether iOS caches assistance data offline, or for how long.
- I found no measured battery-drain figures (mAh/h) for continuous GNSS, VPS or PDR on 2026 phones.
- I did not verify whether IndoorAtlas or other magnetic SDKs run fully offline with a cached map.
